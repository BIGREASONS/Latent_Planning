# Phase 9 — Experimental Validity Audit

> Assume every script runs perfectly. Is the experiment still valid? **Largely no.** The construct has four foundational problems that no amount of clean code fixes.

## E1 — The "teacher" never plans; it reads a provided answer — FATAL to the framing

`run_phase_a.py:301` loads **base** `TinyLlama-1.1B` (`load_model`, no fine-tuning — confirmed: zero use of `load_checkpoint`/`train_teacher` in the pipeline). The text fed to it is the dataset's **ground-truth solution** generated by `generate_countdown_dataset.py`, not anything the model produced. So:
- The hidden states are the LM's encoding of **given-correct text it is reading**, not traces of the model **searching or planning**.
- "Can we roll the latent state forward like a planner?" is mismatched: there is no planner. We are asking whether a frozen autoregressive encoding of pre-written arithmetic can be advanced by an MLP. A positive result would say something about representational geometry, **not** about latent planning.

The paper-level claim ("latent planning in frozen LMs") cannot be supported by this design even with perfect code.

## E2 — The dataset is not Countdown and the task is trivial/degenerate — FATAL to generality

`generate_countdown_dataset.py:11-57` builds problems by a **random forward walk**:
- `target = selected[0]`, then repeatedly `current_val = current_val OP next` and the **final accumulator becomes the target**.
- Example from `data/train.jsonl`: `numbers=[75,100,50,25,3,4]`, solution `["50*100=5000","5000+3=5003"]`, **target=5003**. Real Countdown targets are 100–999; here they are unbounded (5003). Three of the six "numbers" (75, 25, 4) are **never used** — pure distractors.
- Division is never generated (`:31` only `+ − *`), so `Op.DIV` (a quarter of Probe C's classes) **never appears in data** → that class is vacuous.
- Every problem has exactly one trivially-constructed linear solution; there is **no search, no branching, no difficulty**. The "OOD" split differs only by having 5 ops instead of 2–4 (`:23`), i.e. longer chains, not a different distribution.

A representation/dynamics result on this toy says nothing about reasoning.

## E3 — Depth/position is an uncontrolled confound that the probes ride on — MAJOR

Probe B (distance) and Probe D (reachable-in-2) are deterministic functions of token position (metric_inflation §M3). The frozen LM encodes position strongly. The repo *knows* this — Phase C.1/C.2/C.3 exist solely to chase the depth confound — yet Phase A's verdict counts B/D as "reasoning information" (`run_phase_a.py:191`). Until depth is partialled out, "information is linearly decodable" is confounded with "position is decodable."

## E4 — Determinism confound makes Markovianity partly tautological — MAJOR

Because the LM is deterministic and causal, identical text prefixes yield identical hidden states (leakage §L3). Phase C's central question ("is the hidden state a stable Markovian function of the symbolic state?") is confounded: same symbolic state frequently arises from same text → similarity 1.0 by construction, not by representational stability. The `same_hist` mask doesn't remove shared prefixes.

## E5 — Missing controls and baselines — MAJOR
None of the following exist anywhere:
- **Permutation/shuffled-label probe control** (to set a real null for n=63).
- **Raw input-token-embedding probe baseline** (to show the signal lives in *computation*, not in the literal operand tokens sitting in the prompt — critical, since the operands are *right there in the text* the probe state attends to).
- **Random-projection / random-features baseline** for retrieval and probes.
- **Multiple seeds / confidence intervals** — everything is `seed=0` single-shot (`train_*Config.seed=0`).
- A baseline LM **not** trained on the data, or a randomized-weights control beyond the `--smoke` flag.

## E6 — The decoder diagnostic is redundant with the model's own head — MODERATE
`DiagnosticDecoder` (`models/diagnostic_decoder.py`) learns hidden→next-token. But the frozen LM **already has** an unembedding that does exactly this; a linear probe trained for 30 epochs is a weaker copy of it. "Next-token decodability survives rollout" mostly measures how close a rolled-out vector stays to the LM's output cone, not whether reasoning is preserved.

## E7 — Verdict logic encodes the desired conclusion — MODERATE
`run_phase_a.py:236-261` hard-codes a 2×2 decision ("Promising / representation-limited / dynamics-limited / not yet") keyed on arbitrary thresholds (cosine ≥ 0.9 horizon > 3; "encoded" rule). The thresholds were chosen, not derived; with the actual smoke numbers (cosine 0.43, dynamics_gain<1 by depth 3) the machinery would output "not yet / both bottlenecks," which is the honest read — but the thresholds are unjustified and could be tuned to any verdict.

## What is methodologically sound
- The **identity baseline** and **action-blind** ablation (`run_phase_a.py:323-331`) are genuinely good controls — they correctly reveal (M4) that the transition model barely beats identity.
- The **Oracle** isolates representation vs dynamics in principle (a sound idea), even if its sanity check is trivial.
- Train/val/test dedup at problem level exists.

## Verdict
Even with flawless execution, the experiment cannot substantiate a "latent planning" claim: the teacher isn't planning (E1), the task isn't reasoning (E2), and the two headline probes are position detectors (E3) on a deterministic representation where Markovianity is partly tautological (E4), with no negative controls (E5). The good ablations (identity/blind/oracle) are the parts worth keeping — and they point *against* the hypothesis.
